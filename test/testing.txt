Testing is one of the most important parts of building any piece of software. When developers skip testing, small bugs slip through and turn into big problems later. That’s why our team spends the first hour of every morning testing the latest build. We start with unit testing, then move on to integration testing once the individual pieces look solid.

Last week, while testing the file reader, I noticed that it crashed whenever the input was empty. It was frustrating, but that’s exactly what testing is for. After fixing the bug, I kept testing with larger and stranger files until I was confident it could handle anything. My manager joked that I was testing the program’s patience more than its correctness.

Outside of work, testing shows up everywhere. My friend is testing a new recipe for bread this weekend, and my sister is testing for her driver’s license next month. Even the sound engineer at last night’s concert spent ten minutes testing the microphones before the band came on stage. In a way, life itself is just one long round of testing, trying things and learning from what breaks.
